OVERVIEW

The Radrians are an original, open species native to the forests, peaks, and deserts of Esterasul. Their society is built around family, communal living, shared knowledge, and a deep respect for the natural world.

ORIGIN

Family, Clans & Settlement

Radrian society begins with the clan: a close-knit family group built around cooperation, shared responsibility, and survival.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians organize themselves into close-knit family groups or clans, which form the foundation of their social structure. Most clans consist of around 10 to 20 members, while larger clans can grow to approximately 30 individuals.

Clan members are highly interdependent and share responsibilities to ensure the survival and prosperity of the group. Hunting, gathering, child-rearing, education, shelter, and other necessities are treated as communal responsibilities rather than belonging exclusively to individual members.

Most Radrian clans are nomadic, moving seasonally in search of food, water, and other resources. This mobility allows them to adapt to changing environmental conditions while avoiding the overuse of local resources.

Some Mountain Radrian clans differ from this tradition. In areas with reliable resources, they may establish semi-permanent settlements and develop stronger connections to the surrounding land. Some settled clans cultivate fields or develop specialized crafts suited to their region.

Despite their strong familial bonds, Radrian clans rarely use formal names. Their language is simple and heavily dependent on context, so groups are more commonly identified through references to places they frequent or inhabit, such as “the people of the river bend” or “the clan near the towering rocks.”

CLASSIFICATION

Family Roles & Leadership

Radrian clans share responsibilities collectively, while family leadership traditionally rests with the eldest female and spiritual guidance with a shaman.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Family is central to Radrian culture. Responsibilities within a clan are shared among its members, and gender roles are generally fluid rather than strictly divided.

Leadership within a family traditionally falls to the eldest female. This custom developed from the naturally dominant and nurturing role females commonly hold within Radrian society. The eldest female serves as the head of the family, combining authority with the experience and wisdom accumulated over a lifetime.

Clans are also often guided by a shaman. Rather than being a political ruler, the shaman serves as the spiritual and intellectual leader of the group and is typically its wisest member.

Shamans possess extensive knowledge of medicinal herbs, local ecology, traditions, and the surrounding environment. Their knowledge makes them essential to the clan's survival and decision-making.

They may mediate conflicts, advise the clan during difficult decisions, treat injuries and illnesses, and preserve the stories and traditions passed down through generations.

Shamans therefore occupy a uniquely respected position. Their authority comes primarily from knowledge, experience, and trust rather than formal political power.

SOCIETY

Communal Living & Names

Radrian life revolves around shared homes, communal child-rearing, experiential education, and names chosen for their meaning.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians live communally, often residing in large shared houses made from locally available materials. These dwellings are designed to accommodate entire clans and reflect the importance Radrians place on togetherness.

Children are raised by the clan as a whole. While parents remain important figures in a child's life, education and upbringing are communal responsibilities. The younger generation is taught the skills, knowledge, and traditions necessary to survive and contribute to clan life.

Education is informal and experiential. Children learn through storytelling, observation, hands-on activities, and shared responsibilities. Respect for elders and participation in the well-being of the clan are taught from an early age.

Radrian names are closely connected to their language and cultural values. Names are traditionally chosen for their meaning or the positive feelings they evoke, often carrying associations with good fortune.

A child might receive a name equivalent to “sun place,” symbolizing a life free from hardship or darkness. The exact naming system remains flexible and continues to develop.

Over time, contact with humans has influenced Radrian naming traditions. Some Radrians have adopted human words as personal names simply because they like their sound, allowing human-derived names to exist alongside traditional Radrian ones.

SYMBOLISM & FOLKLORE

The World Remembers

Radrian folklore is built around nature, memory, warnings, and the belief that the world retains traces of those who came before.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrian folklore is rich and varied, with traditions differing between clans and regions. Unlike cultures whose myths revolve around gods or distant divine beings, Radrian stories are primarily concerned with natural phenomena, ancestral memory, and cautionary tales.

A recurring idea throughout Radrian folklore is that the world remembers.

A mountain remembers those who crossed it. A river remembers those who disappeared within it. A forest remembers those who passed beneath its trees. And the wind carries what the living have forgotten.

Many old stories began as practical warnings passed from elders to children through songs and tales before gradually becoming legends. Whether unusual events are truly supernatural or simply natural phenomena given meaning is rarely agreed upon.

What matters most is the lesson contained within the story.

THE MOUNTAIN REMEMBERS

Mountain Radrians speak of remembering passes - narrow mountain passages where strong winds create strange whistles, groans, and almost voice-like sounds.

According to legend, an ancient migration attempted to cross the mountains during a brutal winter. Snow buried the paths, rocks gave way, and many travelers never reached the other side. The story says that their voices remained within the mountains.

When the wind passes through the rocks, the dead are said to warn travelers: turn back, do not continue, the mountain has already taken enough. The voices are not considered hostile, but rather warnings from those who learned the danger of the mountains too late.

An old saying captures the lesson: “The mountain does not forget a fallen foot.”

BLACK WATER

Black water refers to deep, dark bodies of water whose bottoms cannot be seen. Deep water is traditionally associated with danger, particularly because most Radrians are poor swimmers.

Stories tell of creatures beneath the surface capable of pulling travelers under. Some versions describe physical animals, while others claim that black water itself remembers everyone who has drowned within it.

This folklore contributes to practical behavior. Radrians generally avoid settling close to deep water and traditionally fish from the shoreline using nets rather than entering the water themselves.

One common tale tells of a thirsty traveler who drank from a mysterious black pool despite ancestral warnings. The next morning, his companions found only his footprints beside the water. His body was never recovered.

The lesson is simple: not everything that looks like salvation is safe to approach.

THE WHISPERING TREES

Forest Radrians tell stories of ancient trees that whisper warnings through their branches. Creaking wood, rustling leaves, and shifting branches are said to carry messages to those capable of listening.

The trees are believed to have witnessed migrations, deaths, storms, predators, and entire clans disappearing into the forest.

A sudden whisper may signal a nearby predator. A strange creak may announce an approaching storm. Sometimes the forest becomes completely silent, which is considered even more concerning.

Shamans are traditionally regarded as especially capable of interpreting these signs, although whether they truly hear something supernatural or simply understand the forest better than others remains uncertain.

The lesson remains: listen before you move.

THE SINGING STONES

Certain rocks and narrow mountain formations can produce faint tones when strong winds pass through them. Radrian folklore calls these formations singing stones.

Some believe the stones contain memories of everyone who has crossed the mountains. Others believe the sounds are the voices of ancestors remaining within the peaks.

Travelers may leave small offerings near particularly famous stones as gestures of respect and remembrance rather than offerings to a deity.

THE GHOST TRAILS

Nomadic clans tell stories of Ghost Trails - faint paths that appear through otherwise untouched wilderness.

Some stories describe them as remnants of routes once used by forgotten clans. Others claim they are paths created by the spirits of travelers who died while searching for safety.

Some Ghost Trails supposedly lead travelers toward shelter or water. Others lead somewhere they should never go.

Because of this, an unfamiliar trail is traditionally approached with caution. Travelers are expected to study their surroundings before following it rather than assuming every path was meant for them.

TERMINOLOGY

Words, Names & Cultural Terms

Radrian terminology is closely connected to their simple, context-driven language and the meanings attached to places, people, and natural phenomena.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Because Radrian language relies heavily on context, clans traditionally do not require formal family names. Instead, groups can be identified by descriptions of places associated with them.

Examples include expressions equivalent to “the people of the river bend” or “the clan near the towering rocks.”

The same principle appears throughout Radrian culture. Names and terms often describe an idea, place, feeling, or characteristic rather than serving only as arbitrary labels.

Important cultural terms include black water, Whispering Trees, singing stones, remembering passes, and Ghost Trails. The exact Radrian-language forms of these terms can be developed alongside the language archive.

Personal names may also be borrowed from human languages. Such names are often adopted simply because a Radrian likes the way a particular human word sounds.

DESIGN GUIDE

Making Your Own

Radrians are an open species. Anyone may create their own character, clan, or interpretation while keeping the core traits of the species.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Esterasul is a collaborative world, and Radrians are designed as an open species. Creators are free to develop their own Radrian characters and clans while respecting the established core traits.

Regional traits provide starting points rather than rigid biological rules. Subspecies characteristics, colours, horn shapes, decorations, and cultural influences can be mixed across regions.

Migration and mixed heritage are both canon, meaning a Radrian does not need to perfectly match the typical appearance or customs of a single region.

When designing a character, consider their environment, clan, family, traditions, occupation, and relationship with the natural world. A Mountain Radrian might have cultural connections to singing stones, while a Southern Radrian may place particular importance on handcrafted jewelry.

For anatomical references, see the Anatomy page. For regional traits and variations, see Subspecies. The Language page contains information about Radrian speech, naming, and terminology.

A Note on Open Design

Esterasul is a collaborative world. Radrians can be designed by anyone following the core traits outlined above. Subspecies, colours, horn shapes, and regional influences are free to mix across regions - migration and mixed heritage are both canon. Use the Subspecies page for regional starting points and the Anatomy page for structural reference.
