Biological Reference

This section is a living reference for Radrian biology. Details here apply broadly across subspecies; regional differences are noted where relevant, and cross-referenced on the Subspecies page.

ANATOMY

Body

Radrians have a tall, elegant, animal-like build with long limbs, necks, and tails. Their digitigrade legs and forward-tilted posture give them a light, poised appearance, while broad shoulders and a strong neck keep them from looking fragile.

dfrggr
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrian's silhouette immediately stands out as tall, elegant, and distinctly animal-like. Their build is elongated rather than bulky, with long limbs, a long neck, and a long tail that all extend well beyond human proportions. Despite this, they never look fragile. Their frame is supported by wide, squared shoulders and a thick, strong neck, which gives even the leaner subspecies a solid sense of weight and presence.

Their stance is one of the most obvious things that separates them from humans. Radrians stand on the balls of their feet, with their heels permanently lifted off the ground. This makes their legs look long and springy, with a noticeable bend partway down instead of the straighter shape of a human leg. It also gives their movements a light, slightly coiled quality, as if they are always ready to run or leap.

Below the waist, the pelvis is relatively long and sits at a slight forward tilt rather than being level like a human pelvis. This changes the balance of the lower body, shifting it slightly forward and down. The position of the hips works together with the digitigrade legs, while the spine and tail follow the same subtle angle. As a result, the body feels like one continuous, flowing shape from the shoulders all the way to the tip of the tail rather than a series of separate sections.

The tail is long and thick at the base. It is usually held fairly straight and extended instead of hanging loosely behind them. It helps counterbalance the tilted pelvis and raised heels, shifting slightly whenever the Radrian changes their weight. In that sense, it works almost like a tightrope walker's balancing pole. Even when standing still, the tail rarely becomes completely limp. There is usually some tension in it, ready to make a small adjustment whenever needed.

Radrian looks less like a heavily built predator and more like a long-limbed dancer. Their body is lean, poised, and naturally suited to balance and quick movement. The wide shoulders and thick neck keep that elegance from making them look delicate, giving the entire silhouette a strong and grounded presence.

Antler Crown & Growth

The crown is grown and shed annually, thickening with age. Growth begins each spring beneath a velvet-like skin that is rubbed away once the antler hardens. Crown shape is inherited but always individually unique.

Growth cycle: annual · Material: keratin-bone composite · Shedding: late winter
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Every Radrian crown begins the same way: a soft, velvet-skinned bud that emerges in early spring and hardens into keratin-bone composite over several months. The final shape is influenced by lineage and region, but no two crowns branch identically, making the crown the single most reliable way to identify an individual at a glance.

Crowns are shed in late winter, usually within a few days across an entire clan — a synchronised event that keepers and Radrians alike treat as a small seasonal milestone. Regrowth the following spring is typically slightly larger and more complex than the year before, which is why crown size is used informally to estimate age.

Pelage & Coloration

Coat density and colour track directly to region — a rule of thumb keepers use when designing new Radrians. Undercoats shed seasonally in all but the tundra variant, which retains a double coat year-round.

Layers: guard hair + undercoat · Moult: twice yearly (except tundra)
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A Radrian's coat is made of two layers: a coarser outer guard hair that sheds water and light, and a softer undercoat that provides insulation. Most subspecies moult this undercoat twice a year, in spring and autumn, while Tundra Radrians retain their thick double coat year-round to survive the Glassfrost's cold.

Coloration correlates closely with region — pale and sand-toned along the coast and dunes, deep umber and moss beneath the forest canopy, frost-white on the tundra — which keepers use as a practical starting point when designing a new Radrian's palette, even for mixed-heritage individuals.

Sensory Organs

Large, independently rotating ears give near-360° hearing, while a Radrian's eyes sit slightly to the sides of the skull for a wide field of view at the cost of some binocular depth perception directly ahead.

Hearing: exceptional · Sight: wide field, moderate depth · Smell: strong, used in clan recognition
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A Radrian's ears rotate almost independently of one another, giving near-360° coverage and making hearing the single sharpest sense across every subspecies. Eyes sit slightly toward the sides of the skull, trading some depth perception directly ahead for an unusually wide overall field of view — useful for spotting predators from nearly any angle.

Scent plays an important social role as well as a practical one: clan members recognise one another partly by scent markers left at shelter thresholds, and a stranger's scent is often noticed before they're ever seen.

Vocal & Communication Organs

Radrians communicate through a mix of low resonant calls, ear posture, and crown angling. Tundra populations have an enlarged vocal chamber that lets their calls travel unusually far across open ice.

Range: low-frequency resonant calls · Secondary: ear & crown posture
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Vocal communication among Radrians leans low and resonant rather than sharp or high-pitched, well suited to travelling through dense forest or open, wind-scoured terrain alike. Tundra Radrians carry an enlarged vocal chamber that lets their calls travel for miles across the flat ice, a trait unmatched by any other subspecies.

Much day-to-day communication happens silently, through ear rotation and the angle at which a Radrian holds its crown — a lowered, forward crown is a common warning posture recognised across every clan, regardless of region.

Digestive System & Diet

As browsing omnivores, Radrians favour bark, lichen, and root vegetables, supplemented seasonally with insects and shellfish along the coast. A multi-chambered stomach allows slow fermentation of tougher plant matter.

Diet: omnivorous, browsing · Stomach: multi-chambered · Seasonal diet shift: yes
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians are browsing omnivores with a multi-chambered stomach that ferments tougher plant matter slowly, similar in principle to a ruminant, letting them extract nutrition from bark and lichen that would otherwise be indigestible. Diet shifts noticeably with the seasons and the region a clan calls home.

Coastal clans supplement their diet with foraged tide-crab, Sunreach clans rely more heavily on root vegetables and the occasional Sandrunner Lizard, and Marsh clans favour the reeds and tubers native to the Marrowfen. Nothing about the digestive system prevents cross-region diets — it's largely a matter of availability.

Reproductive Cycle

Radrians bond for life within their clan, with calves born in early summer after a long gestation. Calves grow their first, small crown within the first year, shedding it annually as they mature.

Gestation: roughly 9 months · Litter size: usually 1 · Maturity: 3–4 years
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians typically bond for life within their clan, and calves are born in early summer after a gestation of roughly nine months, almost always as a single calf rather than a litter. A newborn calf is mobile within hours but stays close to its bonded family group for the first year.

A calf's first crown emerges within that first year as a small, simple bud — nothing like the branching structure it will eventually grow — and is shed and regrown annually from then on, gradually taking on the shape that will identify the individual for the rest of their life.

Lifespan & Aging

A wild Radrian typically lives 40–60 years, with crown complexity increasing steadily with age — elder Radrians are often identifiable by crowns so branched they resemble bare winter trees.

Average lifespan: 40–60 years · Aging marker: crown complexity
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A wild Radrian typically lives between 40 and 60 years, with Mountain and Tundra clans trending toward the higher end thanks to smaller predator pressure at altitude and on the open ice. Crown complexity is the clearest visible marker of age, increasing steadily year over year.

By their final decade, an elder Radrian's crown is often so densely branched it resembles a bare winter tree — a look clans treat with particular reverence, and one many keepers deliberately reference when designing an elder character.
