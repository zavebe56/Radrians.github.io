Biological Reference

This section is a living reference for Radrian biology. Details here apply broadly across subspecies; regional differences are noted where relevant, and cross-referenced on the Subspecies page.

ANATOMY

Body

Radrians have a tall, elegant, animal-like build with long limbs, necks, and tails. Their digitigrade legs and forward-tilted posture give them a light, poised appearance, while broad shoulders and a strong neck keep them from looking fragile.

＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrian's silhouette immediately stands out as tall, elegant, and distinctly animal-like. Their build is elongated rather than bulky, with long limbs, a long neck, and a long tail that all extend well beyond human proportions. Despite this, they never look fragile. Their frame is supported by wide, squared shoulders and a thick, strong neck, which gives even the leaner subspecies a solid sense of weight and presence.

Their stance is one of the most obvious things that separates them from humans. Radrians stand on the balls of their feet, with their heels permanently lifted off the ground. This makes their legs look long and springy, with a noticeable bend partway down instead of the straighter shape of a human leg. It also gives their movements a light, slightly coiled quality, as if they are always ready to run or leap.

Below the waist, the pelvis is relatively long and sits at a slight forward tilt rather than being level like a human pelvis. This changes the balance of the lower body, shifting it slightly forward and down. The position of the hips works together with the digitigrade legs, while the spine and tail follow the same subtle angle. As a result, the body feels like one continuous, flowing shape from the shoulders all the way to the tip of the tail rather than a series of separate sections.

The tail is long and thick at the base. It is usually held fairly straight and extended instead of hanging loosely behind them. It helps counterbalance the tilted pelvis and raised heels, shifting slightly whenever the Radrian changes their weight. In that sense, it works almost like a tightrope walker's balancing pole. Even when standing still, the tail rarely becomes completely limp. There is usually some tension in it, ready to make a small adjustment whenever needed.

Radrian looks less like a heavily built predator and more like a long-limbed dancer. Their body is lean, poised, and naturally suited to balance and quick movement. The wide shoulders and thick neck keep that elegance from making them look delicate, giving the entire silhouette a strong and grounded presence.

Sensory Organs

Large, independently rotating ears give near-360° hearing, while forward-facing eyes provide strong binocular vision, depth perception, and excellent night vision. A strong sense of smell also plays an important role in both hunting and social recognition.

Hearing: exceptional · Sight: excellent night vision, strong depth perception · Smell: strong, used in clan recognition
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A Radrian's ears can rotate almost independently of one another, giving them near-360° auditory coverage and making hearing one of their sharpest senses. Their ears can quickly orient toward sounds from different directions, allowing them to detect movement even when it is outside their direct field of view.

As predators, Radrians have forward-facing eyes that provide strong binocular vision and accurate depth perception. Their eyes are particularly well adapted to low-light environments, giving them excellent night vision and allowing them to hunt and navigate effectively after dark. Their vision remains useful in daylight as well, but darkness does not significantly limit their ability to see.

Scent plays an important social role as well as a practical one. Clan members recognise one another partly by scent markers left at shelter thresholds, and a stranger's scent is often noticed before they are ever seen. Individual scent can therefore provide information about familiarity and social identity, while an unfamiliar scent may immediately draw attention.

Teeth

When the mouth is closed, a Radrian's teeth are almost completely concealed, giving the face a surprisingly human-like appearance. They have flexible pinkish lips and a wide, triangular philtrum, allowing them to spread their lips and expose their entire dentition without opening the mouth. Their teeth have a primitive, sabre-like form, with a sharp cutting edge specialised for tearing meat into manageable pieces.

Dentition: 26 teeth · Primary function: tearing meat · Special feature: sabre-like cutting edges
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A Radrian's teeth are divided into incisors, canines, and postcanine teeth, although the individual teeth share a similar sharp, blade-like shape. Rather than being designed primarily for crushing or grinding, the entire dentition is specialised for gripping and tearing meat into pieces small enough to swallow. Food is then processed further within the digestive system and gizzard.

The upper jaw contains six incisors, two canines, and six postcanine teeth. The lower jaw contains four incisors, two canines, and six postcanine teeth. The canines are particularly prominent and sharp, while the postcanine teeth retain the same general cutting shape rather than developing broad grinding surfaces.

Radrian lips are highly flexible and can spread considerably without the jaw opening. This allows a Radrian to expose all of its teeth at once, whether for feeding, threat displays, or communication. When relaxed, however, the lips cover the teeth almost completely, making the closed mouth appear much more human-like.

The tongue is long, flexible, and leaf-shaped. Its unusual shape helps manipulate pieces of food within the mouth and guides them toward the throat during swallowing. Since Radrians tear their food into pieces rather than extensively chewing it, the tongue plays an important role in positioning food for swallowing.

Horns

The Radrian crown is an important sensory and thermoregulatory organ located on the forehead. Horns always point upward and curve slightly backward, containing an extensive network of blood vessels, nerves, and thermoreceptors that help regulate body temperature and detect subtle changes in the surrounding environment.

Function: thermoregulation & temperature sensing · Structure: vascular soft tissue · Shape: upward pointing, slightly backward curved
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrian horns, traditionally referred to collectively as the crown, are specialised organs rather than simple structures of bone or keratin. They contain numerous small blood vessels, nerves, and thermoreceptors, allowing them to perform both thermoregulatory and sensory functions. The horns always point upward and curve slightly backward, giving the crown its characteristic shape.

The extensive network of blood vessels within the horns helps Radrians regulate their body temperature. When a Radrian needs to cool down, the vessels dilate, allowing increased blood flow through the horns and helping excess heat dissipate into the surrounding environment. In colder conditions, the vessels constrict to reduce heat loss and help maintain a stable internal temperature.

The horns are also highly sensitive to temperature. Numerous thermoreceptors allow Radrians to detect subtle changes in their surroundings, including nearby sources of heat and colder areas. This allows them to sense thermal gradients and respond to changes in their environment before those changes become significant enough to affect the rest of the body.

Because the horns contain living soft tissue and extensive networks of blood vessels and nerves, they can be wounded and are vulnerable to serious injury. Damage may interfere with both temperature regulation and sensory perception. If a horn is severely injured or cut off, the damaged area is often protected with a specialised protective cap while it heals.

Gender Dimorphism

Radrians exhibit very little visible sexual dimorphism. Although their biology includes five primary chromosomal sexes and two rare triploid combinations, these genetic differences have surprisingly little effect on outward appearance. In most cases, it is difficult to determine whether a Radrian is male or female from appearance alone.

Dimorphism: very slight · Chromosomal sexes: 5 primary + 2 triploid · Typical male/female traits: variable · Breasts: absent
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians have five chromosomal sexes, with two additional rare combinations resulting from a triple chromosome mutation. Despite this unusual chromosomal system, chromosomal sex does not directly determine gender identity, and it has only a limited influence on physical appearance. Most of the differences between Radrian sexes are internal or related to reproductive anatomy rather than obvious external traits.

For simplicity, individuals with typically male genitalia are generally referred to as males, while individuals with typically female genitalia are generally referred to as females. This terminology describes physical sex characteristics and does not necessarily correspond to a Radrian's gender identity.

Visible dimorphism is generally limited to subtle differences in body structure and coat growth. Males may develop a more pronounced mane at the base of the tail, while females typically have a smoother tail without this feature. Females may also have slightly wider hips, although the difference is usually subtle and is not a reliable way of identifying sex.

Among the Forest Radrian subspecies, males may develop pronged horns while females generally do not. This trait is not universal, however, and can vary depending on an individual's genes. Horn size and shape also vary naturally between individuals, so the presence or absence of prongs should not be treated as an absolute rule.

These characteristics are tendencies rather than strict biological divisions. A male may have little or no tail mane, while a female may possess features normally associated with males. Genetic variation can produce considerable overlap between individuals, making visual identification of sex unreliable.

Radrians do not have breasts, and there are no major differences in overall body proportions comparable to the pronounced sexual dimorphism seen in many other species. Even the difference in hip width is generally difficult to notice without direct comparison.

Chromosomal Sex

The Radrian sex chromosome system consists of the N and K chromosomes. The most common combinations produce individuals with typically male or female physical characteristics, while other combinations can produce the same physical sex despite having different chromosomal configurations.

NN Male: Two copies of the N chromosome. These individuals develop typically male physical characteristics and are normally fertile.

NK Female: One N chromosome and one K chromosome. These individuals develop typically female physical characteristics and are normally fertile.

NN Female: Two N chromosomes, but a genetic variation affecting the development pathway results in typically female physical characteristics. These individuals are fertile and can reproduce.

NK Male: One N chromosome and one K chromosome, but a genetic variation affecting the K-linked developmental pathway results in typically male physical characteristics. These individuals are fertile and can reproduce.

N0 Male: A single N chromosome without a K chromosome. These individuals develop typically male physical characteristics but are infertile.

Triploid Individuals: Rare triploid Radrians possess three sex chromosomes rather than the usual two. Triploid individuals are infertile regardless of their particular chromosomal combination.

NNN Male: A triploid individual with three copies of the N chromosome. These individuals develop typically male physical characteristics but are infertile.

NNK Female: A triploid individual with two N chromosomes and one K chromosome. These individuals develop typically female physical characteristics but are infertile.

Gender & Identity

Chromosomal sex and gender identity are separate concepts in Radrian society. A Radrian's chromosomes do not determine how they understand or identify themselves, and the biological classification of an individual does not necessarily correspond to their personal sense of gender.

Traditional Radrian culture does not assign strict gender roles. Their language is largely gender-neutral, and everyday social expectations are generally not divided according to sex. Radrians may nevertheless have personal preferences regarding their appearance, presentation, or the pronouns used for them, particularly when interacting with human cultures.

Human influence has introduced additional concepts of gender into some Radrian communities, making it entirely possible for an individual Radrian to develop or adopt an individual gender identity. These identities can vary from person to person and are not determined by their chromosomal sex or physical characteristics.

Coat & Coloration

A Radrian's coat is made of fine, hair-like scales called protofeathers. Most of the body is covered in a short, dense, velvety coat that is smooth and slightly shiny, while longer protofeathers form a more rigid mane along the head, neck, and tail.

Coat: fine protofeathers · Main coat: short, dense & hydrophobic · Mane: long, stiff & expressive
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

The majority of a Radrian's body is covered by short protofeathers that form a dense, velvety layer. This coat is smooth and slightly shiny, with tightly packed scales that help repel water and keep the skin dry. Its density also provides insulation while protecting the skin from insects, abrasions, and minor scratches.

A second type of protofeather grows much longer on the head, neck, and tail. These longer protofeathers form a thick, somewhat stiff mane with a texture closer to a rooster's hackle feathers or a donkey's mane than soft fur. The mane can be raised, flexed, and shaken by the Radrian, making it an important form of visual expression. It is typically a single solid colour, usually appearing as a lighter or darker shade of the main body coat.

Radrian coloration varies considerably between individuals and often provides natural camouflage. Most Radrians have patterns such as stripes, spots, rosettes, or marble-like markings that help break up the outline of the body and blend into their surroundings. The face and underside are typically lighter than the main coat, while common colours include brown, grey, red, beige, and black.

Diet

Radrians are primarily carnivorous opportunistic hunters, favouring fresh meat but remaining capable of eating a variety of other foods. Their diet can be supplemented with fruits, plants, and eggs, while their scavenger ancestry allows them to safely consume carrion when fresh prey is unavailable.

Diet: primarily carnivorous · Feeding style: opportunistic · Digestion aid: gastroliths
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrians are primarily carnivorous and opportunistic hunters. Their ancestors were scavengers, and their digestive systems remain well suited to consuming carcasses when necessary. Modern Radrians are capable of hunting with weapons and tools, however, and generally prefer fresh meat when it is available. They are not strictly dependent on a single type of prey and will take advantage of whatever suitable food is accessible.

Although meat makes up the majority of their diet, Radrians can supplement it with fruits, certain plants, and eggs. Plant-based foods are generally treated as additions rather than staples. Potatoes, grains, and other foods rich in starch are poorly suited to their digestive system when eaten regularly. They can consume them without immediate danger, but a diet heavily reliant on these foods can cause nutritional and digestive problems over time.

Some nuts and other plant foods can be toxic to Radrians, meaning that not every food considered safe for humans is suitable for them. They also digest lactose poorly, making most dairy products difficult for their bodies to process.

Radrians have relatively weak stomach acids, which makes mechanical digestion particularly important. They regularly swallow small stones known as gastroliths, which remain within the digestive tract and help grind and break down tougher pieces of food. Gastroliths are periodically replaced as they become worn or are passed through the digestive system.

The combination of strong mechanical digestion, scavenger ancestry, and an opportunistic feeding strategy allows Radrians to make use of a surprisingly broad range of foods while remaining physiologically adapted to a meat-heavy diet.

Communication

Radrian communication is generally quiet and melodic, combining spoken language with a range of soft vocalizations. Low resonant growls, melodic chirps, and gentle purrs can accompany speech, while expressive body language adds another important layer to everyday communication.

Communication: melodic speech & vocalizations · Secondary: body language & hand symbols
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Radrian languages tend to have a quiet, melodic quality, with spoken communication forming the foundation of everyday interaction. Radrians can also produce a variety of nonverbal vocalizations, including low resonant growls, melodic chirps, and soft purrs. These sounds can accompany speech or be used independently to express emotion, acknowledgement, affection, discomfort, or other subtle meanings.

Body language is equally important to Radrian communication. The position and movement of the tail, the orientation of their expressive ears, facial expressions, and overall posture can all convey information that might otherwise be left unstated. Familiar Radrians are often able to understand these subtle cues without any spoken explanation.

Hand symbols are also commonly used alongside speech. Rather than functioning as a completely separate sign language, these gestures usually accompany spoken words and help emphasize, clarify, or add emotional context to what is being said. Their use can vary between individuals, families, and clans, with some gestures carrying particular cultural meanings.

Because spoken language, vocalizations, facial expressions, and physical gestures are often used together, Radrian communication can convey considerably more information than words alone. A change in tone, ear position, tail movement, or posture may subtly alter the meaning or emotional character of an otherwise ordinary statement.

Reproduction

Radrian reproduction is not restricted by gender or by exclusive sexual partnerships. Radrians may form lifelong romantic bonds while maintaining sexual relationships outside those bonds, and sexual activity is not considered an obligation within a romantic relationship.

Gestation: roughly 16 months · Reproduction: ovoviviparous · Birth: usually 1 calf
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

Gender does not determine who a Radrian may engage in sexual activity with, and Radrians do not generally restrict themselves to a single sexual partner at a time. A Radrian may seek a lifelong romantic partner and form a strong emotional bond with them, but this does not necessarily make the relationship sexually exclusive. Likewise, Radrians do not consider sexual activity an obligation simply because two individuals are romantically bonded.

Impregnation is relatively difficult for Radrians, making reproductive success uncertain from any single pairing. As a result, limiting reproduction to only one sexual partner at a time would place considerable limitations on the species' ability to reproduce. Multiple sexual partners are therefore a normal part of Radrian reproductive behavior.

Radrians are ovoviviparous, with a gestation period lasting approximately 16 months. A newly born Radrian is referred to as a calf. Calves are generally born singly and are cared for by their parents or other responsible adult Radrians within their social group.

Newborn calves are born with a full set of teeth and are capable of eating solid food immediately. Their first foods are generally provided by their parents or other adult caretakers, allowing calves to begin feeding independently while remaining under close protection and care.

Lifespan & Aging

A Radrian typically lives 90–100 years, with social adulthood reached at 20 and full physical maturity reached at 25. Their crown grows longer throughout childhood and adolescence and is typically fully grown by around 25.

Average lifespan: 90–100 years · Social adulthood: 20 years · Full maturity: 25 years
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

A Radrian's early development is relatively slow compared to their long adult lifespan. During childhood, they remain relatively small and are not dramatically different in size from human children. Puberty and rapid physical growth begin around 7–9 years old, bringing a gradual increase in height, limb length, musculature, and overall body proportions.

Growth continues throughout adolescence and gradually slows before ending around the age of 25, when a Radrian reaches full physical maturity. They are socially considered adults at 20 years old, although their bodies continue developing for several years afterward.

The horns, traditionally referred to as a crown, grow longer throughout childhood and adolescence and are typically fully grown by around 25 years old.

Radrians commonly decorate their crowns with jewelry, piercings, bands, chains, and scarifications. Older Radrians tend to have particularly elaborate decorations, with adornments often accumulated throughout their lifetime. These can carry personal, familial, cultural, or social significance, making an elder's crown a visible record of their life and experiences.

A healthy adult Radrian typically lives for 90–100 years, with some individuals surviving beyond this range. Because of their long lifespan, old age is often represented less by a sudden physical decline and more through accumulated crown decorations, changes in the body, and the history expressed through their adornments.
