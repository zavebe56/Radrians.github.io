Language

Words & pronunciation

A working reference for Radric, the shared tongue of the Radrian clans — sounds, structure, and everyday vocabulary.

Overview

Radric is the shared language of the Radrians. Its grammar is compact and context-driven, using word order, suffixes, gestures, and intonation to express meaning. It has no articles, prepositions, or grammatical gender, and its vocabulary includes three distinctive click sounds.

Grammar & Vocabulary

A reference to Radric grammar, core vocabulary, and example sentences.

Grammar

Core Grammar

Word order, pronouns, tense, possession, plurality, negation, questions, and commands.

Word order

The basic word order is subject – verb – object (SVO).

Articles & prepositions

Radric has no articles such as "a" and "the", or prepositions such as "in" and "on". Word order and context convey these relationships.

Pronouns

kaxi = I/me, guka = you (singular), xu = he/she/it, and zotu = they/them. Add -tu to form plural pronouns; kaxitu = we/us.

Tense

There is no separate future tense. Future events are expressed through context or time expressions. The present may use -i; the past may use -a.

kuxui = "he/she/it speaks"   gaxa = "he/she/it saw"

Adjectives

Adjectives usually follow the noun. The suffix -mi can form an adjective from a noun. Meaning can vary with context and gestures.

Possession

Living things commonly use -si on pronouns: kaxisi kuku = "my bird". Objects commonly use -xi on nouns: ǀaqixi = "my house".

Imperative

The bare verb stem is used for commands: ga! = "look!" and ǂoga! = "don't look!"

Plural nouns

Nouns take -tu in the plural: zebi = fish, zebitu = fish. Nouns have no gender.

Negation

Negation is formed by placing ǂo before the verb.

Questions

Questions use rising intonation or ma at the end: Guka xuqu ma? = "Are you sleeping?"

Compound words

Compound words can combine two or more words, such as Gukadaxi, from guka (you) and daxi (run).

A Note on Usage

Radric depends strongly on context. Adjectival meanings may shift with the situation and with gestures. Time expressions establish future meaning without a dedicated future tense, while rising intonation or ma can mark a question.
