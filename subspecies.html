RADRIAN SUBSPECIES

Radrians occur in three recognized regional subspecies. Differences in climate and habitat have shaped their size, proportions, horns, ears, and behavior, while individual height can vary beyond the ranges listed below.

✦
SOUTHERN · DESERT REGIONS

Southern Radrian

The most common Radrian subspecies, known for slender bodies, thin horns, smaller ears, and a deep love of jewelry and decorative craftsmanship.

330–370 cm average height · Desert-adjacent · Most common subspecies
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

The Southern subspecies of Radrians is a fascinating variation known for its affinity for jewelry and intricate craftsmanship. These desert-dwelling Radrians have a penchant for decorating their daily life items and accessories, both for themselves and their companions. Their creativity and attention to detail are evident in the ornate designs that adorn their surroundings.

Southern Radrians have very slim bodies and thin horns. Their ears are noticeably smaller than those of the other subspecies. They usually live close to the desert, where they have adapted to arid and harsh conditions. Their slim bodies allow them to maneuver swiftly through desert terrain, using their agility to navigate sand dunes and the difficult landscape.

The average height for Southern Radrians is 330–370 cm. Individual Radrians can be taller or shorter than this stated range.

One of the most distinctive aspects of Southern Radrian culture is their fondness for jewelry and decorative craftsmanship. They spend significant amounts of time meticulously crafting and embellishing various items, from intricate necklaces and bracelets to designed trinkets, tools, and other objects used in daily life. Their creations serve both practical and aesthetic purposes, reflecting an appreciation for beauty and individual expression.

Their affinity for jewelry extends beyond personal adornment. Southern Radrians often create and exchange accessories as tokens of friendship and social bonding. These pieces can become cherished symbols of connection and camaraderie, strengthening relationships within their communities.

As the most common subspecies of Radrians, Southern Radrians form a widespread and thriving population around the desert regions. Their resourcefulness, creativity, and sociability contribute to their ability to adapt to and flourish in arid environments.

▲
MOUNTAIN · HIGH ALTITUDES

Mountain Radrian

The largest Radrian subspecies, distinguished by massive horns and large ears, yet known for an unusually gentle and laid-back temperament.

340–390 cm average height · Harsh mountain environments · Largest subspecies
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

The Mountain subspecies of Radrians is known for its gentle nature despite being the biggest of the three subspecies. Standing at an average height of 340–390 cm, these Radrians possess a powerful and imposing physique. Individual Radrians can be taller or shorter than the stated range.

Their most eye-catching traits are their large ears and massive horns. The horns can vary in shape and curvature and serve multiple purposes. They are used for regulating body temperature and for display during mating rituals. Within their social structure, the horns can also act as symbols of strength and endurance, with larger horns often indicating higher status and dominance.

Life in harsh mountain environments has shaped a strong physique, muscular build, and sturdy limbs capable of handling rugged terrain. These physical traits can make Mountain Radrians appear intimidating, but their appearance contrasts strongly with their laid-back and tranquil demeanor.

Mountain Radrians are notably docile and peaceful. They are often found engaging in harmonious interactions with other members of their species and even humans. Their large ears enhance their sense of hearing, allowing them to detect faint sounds in the mountainous surroundings.

The challenging environments they inhabit have also given them considerable resilience and adaptability. They have learned to thrive among rugged landscapes and to locate food and freshwater sources at high altitudes.

The Mountain Radrian embodies a striking balance between immense physical strength and a calm, gentle nature, making the subspecies especially distinctive within the Radrian species.

❖
FOREST · NORTHERN CONTINENT

Forest Radrian

A rare northern subspecies with compact, bulky bodies and a lively temperament, often recognized by its distinctive pronged horns.

290–330 cm average height · Dense northern forests · Rare subspecies
＋ Read full entry Collapse entry

The Forest subspecies of Radrians is a rare and elusive group that resides in the dense forests on the northern side of the continent. With an average height ranging from 290–330 cm, they are notably smaller than the other Radrian subspecies while possessing somewhat bulky bodies. Individual Forest Radrians can fall outside the stated height range.

Their compact and sturdy builds are well suited to woodland environments. They navigate lush vegetation and towering trees with agility and precision, using their smaller size and robust bodies to move through dense foliage.

One distinguishing feature of Forest Radrians is their pronged horns. This trait is particularly dominant in males, adding a distinctive complexity to their appearance.

Forest Radrians are an energetic bunch. They tend to be less friendly than their cousins and often display a lively, spirited nature. Their energy can be seen in vigorous activity, playful behavior, acrobatics, and agile movements as they navigate their forest homes.

Living in the solitude of the forest has encouraged a degree of self-sufficiency and independence. Forest Radrians are adept at using their environment's resources for sustenance and shelter, allowing them to thrive within their woodland ecosystem.

Due to their elusive nature and preference for seclusion, encounters with Forest Radrians are relatively infrequent. They maintain a certain level of distance from other creatures, including their own kind, and tend to be more territorial. This behavior, combined with their rarity and northern forest habitat, gives them an enigmatic presence among the Radrian subspecies.
